Company Investigation

What a Company Investigation looks at.

Most company risk shows up first in official records and public sources. deepin.risk reads them continuously and tells you which changes matter.

Capital structure

How a company's capital moves, and whether that movement makes sense.

  • Capital increases and reductions, tracked over time
  • Signs of artificial growth
  • Capital movements compared with sector peers

Partners and authority

Who owns the company and who can sign for it.

  • Notable changes in partners, officers and signatories
  • Foreign partner or officer appointments
  • Shareholding changes above your thresholds
  • Internal directives and signing authorities

Early warning

A multi-dimensional check on new and high-risk companies.

  • Fraud-risk patterns in newly established firms
  • Risky addresses and unusual transaction volumes
  • Frequent branch and agency openings or closures
  • Reputation signals from news, websites and social media

Relationship network

The connections that a single company record hides.

  • People who recur across multiple companies
  • Multi-layered partnerships and group structures
  • Companies clustered at the same address
  • Suspicious person-to-company links

Relationship network

One change. Three connected companies.

A new signatory at one supplier looks routine on its own. deepin.risk follows the people and addresses behind it and finds that the same person is a partner in two companies you already watch, at an address shared by fourteen firms.

That context turns a registry entry into an investigation, with every link traced back to its source.
