The problem

The logs are collected. Reading them takes time.

Firewalls, VPNs, sign-ins and computers produce millions of log entries every day. They are stored, but nobody has the time to read them all, and what matters gets lost in the crowd.

Not enough time

Understanding a single alert means opening five different systems. An analyst's day ends after a handful of alerts.

What matters slips through

An entry that needs attention sits among thousands of ordinary ones and goes unnoticed.

One incident, many alerts

Alerts about the same incident arrive separately. Working out which ones belong together takes hours.

Silent failures

A service fails for days, monitoring checks the wrong place, and nobody notices.

The difference

Rules catch what is known. Deepin sees what is unusual.

Every security rule describes an attack someone has already seen. However long the list gets, what has not been described yet is not on it. That is why Deepin learns what normal looks like, not what attacks look like.

How it works

Every suspicion, the same questions.

Deepin does not run a list of rules. For every suspicious situation it asks, in order, the questions an experienced analyst would ask, and leaves the decision to a person.

  1. Logs

    The logs your systems already collect are read; nothing is changed.

  2. Identity

    Addresses become servers, accounts become people and teams.

  3. Question

    Did this person go somewhere nobody on their team goes?

  4. Comparison

    How many people do the same? What everyone does is not suspicious.

  5. Reasonable explanation

    An update, a test, a monitoring tool? That is checked first.

  6. Case

    Alerts about the same incident come together in one file.

  7. Human decision

    An analyst approves, rejects or takes it further.

  8. Evidence

    Every number in the case is backed by the original log entries.
